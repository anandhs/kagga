## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

All people see one sky and tread one earth.  
They eat the same grain and drink the same water.  
Within humankind, breathing the same air,  
how did this hostility arise, Mankutimma?

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 14 builds its question through elemental repetition. Sky, earth, grain, water, and air are not abstract symbols here; they are the common conditions of every human life. Against that plain fact, hostility appears not natural or inevitable, but strange enough to demand an explanation.

The verse does not erase real differences or promise that recognizing common needs will settle every dispute. Its target is *vaiṣamya*: estrangement, antagonism, or hostile division. DVG's question retains an edge because the shared world has not prevented people from treating one another as enemies.

That tension remains modern. Our dependencies cross the very boundaries that politics, caste, creed, wealth, and nationality can harden. The comic's clay pot makes the contradiction visible: the quarrelers can turn away from each other, but not from the water they share.
