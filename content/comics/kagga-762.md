## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

In this walk upon the earth, let Meru be the goal;  
the day Meru is forgotten, the road leads downward into hell.  
What if it is far away? What if the leg is impaired?  
The memory of home itself is strength, Mankutimma.

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 762 imagines life as an earthly walk oriented toward Meru, the great mountain. Meru is more than a geographical destination: it can signify moral height, spiritual orientation, or the highest condition toward which a life is directed. The verse's severe warning is that forgetting this orientation turns the road itself toward *naraka*—a diminished or hellish mode of living.

Yet DVG does not pretend that the journey is easy. The goal may be far away, and the traveler's leg may be impaired. Neither condition makes the goal unreal. What supplies strength is *the memory of home*: not proof of imminent arrival, but an inward recognition of where one belongs and which direction remains worth taking.

The bodily metaphor needs care today. It should not be reduced to a claim that determination overcomes disability, or that physical limitation represents moral failure. The verse distinguishes capacity from orientation. A person may have only one painful step available; remembering the destination can still keep that step from becoming aimless.
