## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

To find balance amid inequality, friendship amid adversity;  
to weave a guiding thread of harmony through what does not fit;  
to perceive play and delight in sorrow-laden worldly existence—  
that cultivated responsiveness itself is yoga, Mankutimma.

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 241 gathers four difficult acts into one vision: discerning balance amid inequality, creating friendship amid adversity, drawing a thread of reconciliation through what seems incompatible, and remaining capable of delight in a sorrow-filled world.

The final word *rasikate* is richer than simple cheerfulness. It suggests cultivated responsiveness—the aesthetic and humane capacity to perceive relation, proportion, play, and meaning. DVG provocatively calls this *yoga*. The discipline lies neither in escaping disorder nor in pretending that unequal things are identical, but in making a livable concord without erasing difference.

A shallow reading might reduce the verse to “stay positive.” That would miss both its difficulty and its social edge. Laughter does not cancel suffering, and reconciliation does not excuse injustice. The verse instead asks whether sorrow and conflict must be allowed to occupy the whole field of experience.
