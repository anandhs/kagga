## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

When Rama lived, did not Ravana live too?  
When Bhima lived, there was a Dushasana.  
Has this earth ever lacked a doer of injustice?  
Become Rama's ally, Mankutimma.

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 288 begins with two deliberately paired memories from the epics. Rama's presence did not mean Ravana was absent; Bhima's strength did not prevent Dushasana from existing. DVG then turns those examples into a blunt question: has the earth ever known a time without someone who commits injustice?

The final command matters more than the complaint. *Rāmabhaṭa* can mean Rama's soldier, servant, retainer, or follower. “Ally” is used in the working translation to preserve the ethical force without narrowing the line to literal warfare. DVG does not tell Mankutimma to imagine himself as Rama. He asks the ordinary person to decide where he stands and whom his conduct serves.

A shallow reading could use the verse to divide the present into self-proclaimed heroes and demonized enemies. That would evade its demand for self-examination. The names carry moral orientation, but they do not relieve us of judgment, proportion, or restraint. To be on the side of justice may mean an unglamorous intervention: refusing to cooperate with deceit, protecting someone exposed to power, or simply declining to look away.

That edge remains contemporary because unfair systems rarely depend on spectacular villains alone. They also depend on bystanders, habits, and small permissions. The verse offers no fantasy of a purified age; it asks what allegiance becomes visible through one's next ordinary act.

The preferred Kannada follows the official repository, including *ದುಶ್ಶಾಸನನ*. Some secondary transcriptions print *ದುಶ್ಯಾಸನನ*; the difference does not alter the interpretation, but the verified official wording is preserved here.
