## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

Old devotion and faith have faded away.  
No brightness from any new vision has appeared.  
Like a lame, blind person whose familiar house has fallen,  
the world trembles in distress, Mankutimma.

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 15 describes an interval of cultural homelessness. The old structures of *bhakti* and *shraddha*—devotion, faith, trust, and accustomed conviction—have worn away. Yet no new *darshana*, no compelling vision or way of seeing, has arrived with enough light to orient the world.

DVG's image is deliberately severe: a person who depended on intimate knowledge of a familiar house is left helpless when that house collapses. The metaphor reflects the language and assumptions of its time; its philosophical force lies in the loss of dependable orientation, not in treating disability itself as spiritual failure.

A nostalgic reading would simply rebuild the old house. The verse says less and asks more. When inherited authority has failed but a new framework remains unfinished, anxiety is real—and so is the temptation to accept any loud certainty. The waiting community in the comic holds that unresolved threshold open.
