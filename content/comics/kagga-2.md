## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

Whatever it is that surrounds this world of living and inert forms,  
and seems to dwell within it, filling it through and through,  
beyond the grasp of feeling or conception, beyond every measure—  
bow to that extraordinary presence, Mankutimma.

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 2 moves from the names of the unseen source in the opening verse to its relation with the visible world. Living beings and inert matter appear different, yet DVG imagines an unnamed *something* that both surrounds them and fills them from within.

The phrase concerning *bhāva* allows useful breadth: this presence does not become fully captive to feeling, imagination, or conceptual thought. Nor does it fit within measurement. The verse does not belittle emotion or empirical inquiry; it warns against mistaking either one for complete possession of reality.

That distinction remains relevant wherever measurable facts and lived experience meet. Both can disclose the world, while humility leaves room for what their categories have not exhausted. The closing bow is therefore not a substitute for inquiry, but an acknowledgment of its horizon.
