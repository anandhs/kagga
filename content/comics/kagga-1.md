## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

People call it Sri Vishnu—the source from which the universe began, delighting in Māyā;  
God, Lord of all, the Supreme Brahman.  
Though they have not seen it, they believe in it with deep affection.  
Bow to that wonder, Mankutimma.

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 1 opens as an invocation, but its movement is subtler than a declaration of one settled name. People call the unseen source *Sri Vishnu*, the beginning of the universe, the one who delights in or moves through Māyā, God, Lord of all, and the Supreme Brahman. The verse gathers these descriptions and then ends by asking Mankutimma to bow to **that wonder**.

The final phrase matters. DVG does not pretend that naming the source is the same as seeing or comprehending it. People have not seen *that which* they name, yet they hold it with affection and trust. Reverence here can therefore coexist with epistemic humility: the names are meaningful approaches to mystery, not proof that mystery has been exhausted.

A shallow reading might turn the verse into either a demand for blind faith or an argument against belief. It does neither. It observes the strange human act of loving and trusting what cannot be directly seen, while preserving the distance between our descriptions and what they seek to describe.

That tension remains contemporary. Scientific models, religious language, philosophical categories, and personal metaphors can help us approach what exceeds immediate experience. The verse cautions us not to confuse any useful name with complete possession of the truth. Its gesture is neither certainty nor cynicism, but a bow before what remains wondrous.
