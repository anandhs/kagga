## English translation

*Working translation by Kagga Bites, informed by the sources below.*

Do not sink; keep swimming upon life's surface.  
Do good; give and receive joy.  
When the final call comes, go without anxiety.  
Remain mingled with the universe, Mankutimma.

## English explanation and summary

Kagga 750 holds two impulses together. Its opening image asks us not to sink into life's river but to keep swimming at its surface. Yet this buoyancy is not withdrawal: the next line turns outward, toward doing good and toward the reciprocal movement of giving and receiving joy.

The verse then brings death into view through *the final call*. DVG does not promise control over its timing or ask us to deny the grief surrounding it. He asks for a way of walking that does not become panic when departure arrives. The closing phrase, *miḷitaniru viśvadali*, can suggest remaining mingled, joined, or integrated with the world. Detachment therefore does not mean standing apart from life; it means participating without being swallowed by possession, anxiety, or self-importance.

A shallow reading could mistake “stay afloat” for emotional avoidance or “move without anxiety” for indifference. The verse resists both. Its swimmer survives precisely by exchanging help and happiness with others. In modern life, that distinction matters: resilience is not invulnerability, but the ability to remain useful, receptive, and connected while the current remains stronger than any individual.

The preferred text above follows the official Kagga repository and the matching Viveka Jyothi transcription. The Kannada Sahitya commentary prints a small verbal variant in the second line; it is useful for interpretation, but not used to alter the verified text here.
