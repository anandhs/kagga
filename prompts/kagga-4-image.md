# Kagga 4 comic image prompt

Use case: illustration-story

Asset type: final landscape comic strip for a literary blog

Create a new, coherent six-panel watercolor-and-ink comic interpreting Kagga 4 as disciplined openness before the fundamental questions of life, world, relationship, the unseen, and the limits of knowledge. Use the supplied Kagga 1 comic only as a character and art-direction reference. Do not reuse its scenes or central golden void.

Use one wide landscape page with exactly six equal pictures in a clean three-column by two-row grid, separated by narrow warm-paper gutters. Reading order is left to right across the top row, then the bottom row.

Mankuthimma is the same ordinary middle-aged Kannada householder and traveler: slightly untidy graying dark hair, modest moustache, weathered kindly face, attentive intelligent eyes, compact adult proportions, handwoven off-white cotton shirt, traditional panche/dhoti, faded earth-toned shoulder cloth, worn sandals, and practical cloth bag. He is humble, reflective, engaged in ordinary life, religion-agnostic, and spiritually curious without representing any faith—never a monk, guru, or renunciate. A tiny sparrow appears subtly in each panel.

1. Mankuthimma pauses while planting with a farming family and studies a tiny seedling in his palm. Caption: “What does life mean?”
2. He stands before a vast layered world of village, forest, mountains, river, clouds, and stars, beautiful but unreadable. Caption: “What does the world mean?”
3. At a riverbank he sees a fish depend on water, roots hold soil, a bird ride air, and people cross a bridge—the lives and world mutually connected. Caption: “How do they belong together?”
4. At dusk he raises a small lantern; its circle reveals a few stones and roots while most of the landscape remains beyond sight. Caption: “What remains beyond sight?”
5. At night, Mankuthimma holds the same ordinary lantern over a road beside the river. Its small circle reveals a few stones and animal footprints that continue beyond the light into ordinary darkness. Caption: “The lantern showed only a little.”
6. At dawn, Mankuthimma hangs the extinguished lantern from his cloth bag and walks onward along the same road toward an open peach-gold horizon. Caption: “He carried the questions onward.”

Use warm, reflective hand-painted watercolor with loose uneven ink outlines, transparent washes, visible paper texture, soft color blooms, and generous negative space. Palette: off-white cotton, faded rust, muted indigo, seedling green, river blue, warm lantern amber, indigo dusk, and peach dawn. The lantern is ordinary and its light limited; avoid mystical rays, abstract currents, overhead symbolic compositions, and literal question-mark symbols.

Critical constraint: exactly six pictures and exactly one English narration caption per picture. Render the six quoted captions verbatim, in order, in consistent warm-cream caption bands with large dark legible lettering. Do not paraphrase, misspell, duplicate, or omit them. No Kannada, dialogue, speech bubbles, labels, title, panel numbers, logo, watermark, credits, signs, question-mark symbols in the artwork, decorative script, pseudo-writing, or other text.
