# Kagga 2 comic image prompt

Use case: illustration-story

Asset type: final landscape comic strip for a literary blog

Create a new, coherent six-panel watercolor-and-ink comic interpreting Kagga 2 as an immeasurable presence surrounding and filling both living and inert things. Use the supplied Kagga 241 comic only as a wayfarer-character and art-direction reference; do not reuse its market, storm, canopy, or patchwork metaphor.

Use one wide landscape page with exactly six equal pictures in a clean three-column by two-row grid, separated by narrow warm-paper gutters. Reading order is left to right across the top row, then the bottom row.

Mankuthimma is an ordinary middle-aged Kannada wayfarer between destinations. He has slightly untidy graying dark hair, a modest moustache, weathered kindly features, attentive intelligent eyes, and mature adult proportions. He wears a handwoven off-white cotton shirt, traditional white panche/dhoti with a muted border, faded rust shoulder cloth, worn sandals, and an indigo cloth travel bag. Establish no home, family role, occupation, rank, doctrine, or vow. He joins practical activity when useful and learns through uncertain attention rather than spiritual authority. He is neither a settled householder nor a monk, pilgrim, priest, guru, or renunciate. No saffron robe, shaven head, religious or sectarian marks, clerical clothing, prayer beads, guru pose, or childlike features. A tiny sparrow may appear subtly.

1. In a quiet clearing, show vivid living forms—a sapling, butterfly, deer, and sparrow—beside inert forms—a large stone, clay pot, and still sand. Mankuthimma observes both groups while steadying the clay pot for a villager. Caption: “Life moves. Stone rests.”
2. The living and inert forms share the same earth beneath one open sky; roots pass between stones and water touches the clay pot. Caption: “Yet both share one world.”
3. A subtle translucent blue-silver current arcs around the tree, deer, stones, pot, Mankuthimma, and landscape without becoming a deity or figure. Caption: “Something surrounds them all.”
4. In a closer view, that same fine current passes through leaf veins, the stone’s mineral grain, the clay pot, the sparrow, and Mankuthimma’s open palm. Caption: “And fills them from within.”
5. Mankuthimma tries to span the current with a simple measuring cord, but the current continues far beyond both ends into sky and earth. This is the signature image. Caption: “No measure can contain it.”
6. He lowers and releases the cord. With his travel bag still on his shoulder and the onward road visible, he makes a small tentative bow of the head with open empty hands while remaining on his feet as the subtle current flows through and around the clearing. No kneeling, prostration, prayer pose, or worship object. Caption: “Bow to the immeasurable.”

Use warm hand-painted watercolor, loose uneven ink outlines, transparent pigment washes, visible paper texture, soft color blooms, and generous breathing room. Palette: off-white cotton, faded rust, muted indigo, soft sky blue, leafy green, warm gray stone, terracotta, and pale silver-blue for the current. Keep the current restrained and natural, not a magical beam or religious icon.

Critical constraint: exactly six pictures and exactly one English narration caption per picture. Render the six quoted captions verbatim, in order, in consistent warm-cream caption bands with large dark legible lettering. Do not paraphrase, misspell, duplicate, or omit them. No Kannada, dialogue, speech bubbles, labels, title, panel numbers, logo, watermark, credits, signage, decorative script, pseudo-writing, or other text.
