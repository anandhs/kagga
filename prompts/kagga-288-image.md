# Kagga 288 comic image prompt

Use case: illustration-story

Asset type: final landscape six-panel comic for the Kagga Bites literary blog

Use the established Mankuthimma character reference and the collection's expressive Kagga 241 strip as identity and art-direction references. Preserve the same ordinary middle-aged Kannada householder: wavy untidy dark hair touched with gray, modest moustache, weathered kindly face, attentive eyes, subtle age lines, handwoven off-white cotton shirt, white traditional panche/dhoti with muted blue edge, faded rust shoulder cloth, worn sandals, and practical blue-gray cloth bag.

Create a completely original six-panel visual interpretation of Kagga 288. Injustice existed even in the ages of Rama and Bhima; instead of waiting for a flawless world or pretending to be a legendary hero, an ordinary person must choose to assist what is right. Show moral allegiance through modest, concrete, nonviolent action. Do not turn the verse into tribal politics, vigilantism, demonizing opponents, sectarian preaching, worship, or a call to violence.

Use one lively Karnataka village fair at late afternoon, with a traditional leather shadow-puppet performance beside an ordinary grain market. Warm lamplight grows as evening approaches. Keep one elderly woman grain seller, one prosperous middle-aged male grain merchant, and Mankuthimma visually consistent. Use exactly six equal rectangular pictures on one wide landscape page, in a clean three-column by two-row grid with narrow warm-paper gutters. Place exactly one cream caption band at the bottom of every picture.

1. Mankuthimma sits among ordinary villagers watching a traditional backlit leather shadow-puppet screen. Only on the glowing screen, stylized flat puppet silhouettes respectfully suggest Rama with a bow on one side and many-headed Ravana on the other. No combat, sacred icon, shrine, text, or labels. Sparrow on the tent rope. Caption: “Rama’s age still contained a Ravana.”
2. The shadow-puppet scene changes. Only on the screen, stylized flat silhouettes suggest strong Bhima holding a lowered mace and Dushasana opposing him. No combat or gore. Beyond the audience, the real grain market begins to attract Mankuthimma's attention. Caption: “Beside mighty Bhima stood Dushasana too.”
3. At the neighboring grain stall, a prosperous merchant secretly presses one thick thumb onto a brass balance scale, tilting it against an elderly woman seller and shortchanging her. She holds a small grain basket and looks puzzled. Other shoppers notice but avert their eyes. Caption: “No age has emptied itself of injustice.”
4. Mankuthimma stands between the glowing puppet screen and the real grain stall. He is visibly ordinary and uncertain: no weapon, authority, status, heroic pose, or religious symbol. He looks from the mythic shadows to the cheating thumb, then takes one hesitant step forward. Caption: “He was neither king nor legendary warrior.”
5. Tight, simple view of the brass market scale. The merchant's thick thumb presses one pan downward. Mankuthimma's smaller open hand gently but firmly lifts the cheating thumb away; both pans visibly begin to level. The elderly seller and merchant react behind the scale. Make this the unforgettable focal image. Caption: “Still, one small hand could choose a side.”
6. Warm evening. Mankuthimma walks onward through the fair with his cloth bag and sparrow nearby, modestly glancing back. Behind him the brass scale remains perfectly level; the merchant now measures honestly, the elderly seller stands straighter, and formerly passive witnesses remain watching. Caption: “The scale stayed level after he walked on.”
