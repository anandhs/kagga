# Kagga 3 comic image prompt

Use case: illustration-story

Asset type: final landscape comic strip for a literary blog

Create a new, coherent six-panel watercolor-and-ink comic interpreting Kagga 3: an unknowable principle may be roaming through the world in the many guises of life; if its goodness is true, take refuge in that depth. Use the supplied Kagga 1 comic only as a character and art-direction reference. Do not reuse its scenes or central golden void.

Use one wide landscape page with exactly six equal pictures in a clean three-column by two-row grid, separated by narrow warm-paper gutters. Reading order is left to right across the top row, then the bottom row.

Mankuthimma is the same ordinary middle-aged Kannada householder and traveler: slightly untidy graying dark hair, modest moustache, weathered kindly face, attentive intelligent eyes, compact adult proportions, handwoven off-white cotton shirt, traditional panche/dhoti, faded earth-toned shoulder cloth, worn sandals, and practical cloth bag. He is humble, reflective, engaged in ordinary life, religion-agnostic, and spiritually curious without representing any faith—never a monk, guru, or renunciate. A tiny sparrow appears subtly in every panel.

1. At dawn Mankuthimma pauses while carrying vegetables home and examines an empty patch of mist between trees, unsure whether anything is there. Caption: “Is it there—or not?”
2. The mist parts onto an ordinary world of path, water, stones, plants, animals, and people, offering no single visible answer. Caption: “The world offers no answer.”
3. A single pale breeze-like ribbon moves through a sprouting seed, swimming fish, running deer, laughing child, old woman, and sparrow—many living guises, no divine figure. Caption: “Yet life wears countless forms.”
4. Mankuthimma follows the same quiet ribbon as it changes motion with each life while remaining visually continuous. Caption: “One wonder moves through them.”
5. During rain, the ribbon gently guides a fallen fledgling beneath a leaf and bends a branch to shelter a tired traveler, suggesting goodness without sentimental miracle. Caption: “If that goodness is true...”
6. At twilight Mankuthimma shares the sheltering tree with the tired traveler while the ribbon disappears into deep roots and dark earth. End with trust that preserves uncertainty. Caption: “Rest in the hidden depth.”

Signature image: one continuous pale ribbon wearing the movements of many living forms while its own source remains unseen.

Use warm whimsical hand-painted watercolor, loose slightly uneven ink contours, transparent washes, paper texture, soft pigment blooms, and simple uncluttered scenes. Palette: off-white cotton, faded rust, muted indigo, moss green, river blue, rose, warm earth, rain gray, and deep violet roots. The ribbon must be subtle and airy, not a glowing deity, ghost, or superhero effect.

Critical constraint: exactly six pictures and exactly one English narration caption per picture. Render the six quoted captions verbatim, in order, in consistent warm-cream caption bands with large dark legible lettering. Preserve the em dash and three periods exactly. No Kannada, dialogue, speech bubbles, labels, title, panel numbers, logo, watermark, credits, signage, decorative script, pseudo-writing, or other text.
