# Kagga 1 comic image prompt

Use case: illustration-story

Asset type: final landscape comic strip for a literary blog

Create one coherent six-panel watercolor-and-ink comic interpreting the first verse of *Mankuthimmana Kagga* as reverence before an unseen mystery. Include one short English narration caption in each panel.

Use a wide landscape page containing exactly six distinct, equal-sized pictures in a clean three-column by two-row grid, separated by narrow warm-paper gutters. Preserve left-to-right reading order across the top row, then the bottom row.

Mankuthimma is an ordinary middle-aged Kannada householder and traveler, not a monk or renunciate. He has slightly untidy dark hair touched with gray, a modest moustache, weathered kindly features, attentive intelligent eyes, compact adult proportions, and an unassuming posture. He wears a handwoven off-white cotton shirt, traditional panche/dhoti, a faded earth-toned shoulder cloth, worn sandals, and a practical cloth bag. He looks simple at first glance yet deeply reflective, humble, and present in ordinary worldly life. He is religion-agnostic but spiritually curious: open to wonder and respectful of many beliefs without belonging to or representing any faith. He is not DVG or a historical portrait. No saffron robe, shaven monk styling, religious or sectarian mark, clerical clothing, halo, prayer beads, guru pose, or childlike features. A tiny sparrow appears subtly in every panel.

1. In a welcoming village landscape, ordinary people quietly turn toward different natural manifestations: sunrise, an old tree, flowing water, and a small sheltered flame. Mankuthimma walks among them and helps an elder set down a water pot. Caption: “Some call the unseen source Vishnu, Creator, Lord.”
2. Mankuthimma follows their different gazes and sees that each person responds to a different natural form. Keep the scene simple and non-sectarian. Caption: “Many names reach toward the same hidden mystery.”
3. Mankuthimma searches behind the tree, peers into clear water, and looks beyond the flame, finding no visible being. Caption: “No searching eye has ever seen its face.”
4. One invisible breeze simultaneously bends leaves, ripples water, leans the flame, lifts the edge of his robe, and carries the sparrow. Caption: “Still, people trust the unseen with love.”
5. At twilight, leaves, a ribbon of water, a small flame, and stars form a quiet circular motion around a completely empty, softly radiant golden center. Mankuthimma stands at the edge in wonder. The empty luminous negative space is the unmistakable focal point. Caption: “Every form gathers around one nameless mystery.”
6. At dawn, Mankuthimma bows gently before the same luminous emptiness in an open clearing while the sparrow rests nearby. End in quiet humility. Caption: “So he bows to what no name contains.”

Use the lively visual language of a classic hand-drawn Sunday newspaper adventure comic: variable-width black brush-and-pen lines, loose gestural shapes, elastic but adult facial acting, expressive body language, atmospheric watercolor washes, occasional dry-brush texture, and playful natural movement. The result should feel handmade, spirited, warm, intelligent, and contemplative without copying any named artist, copyrighted characters, recognizable character designs, or specific published strip. Use a palette of warm cream paper, off-white cotton, faded rust, muted indigo, soft sky blue, leafy green, warm sienna earth, peach-gold, and lavender twilight. Keep Mankuthimma consistent across all six panels.

Critical constraint: exactly six pictures and exactly one English narration caption per picture. Render the six quoted captions above verbatim, in reading order, using consistent warm-cream caption bands with large, dark, highly legible lettering. Do not paraphrase, misspell, duplicate, or omit any caption. No Kannada script, dialogue, speech bubbles, labels, title, logo, watermark, footer, signage, decorative script, pseudo-writing, panel numbers, parchment scroll, or other text anywhere in the image.
