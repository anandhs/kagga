# Kagga 5 comic image prompt

Use case: illustration-story

Asset type: final landscape comic strip for a literary blog

Create a new, coherent six-panel watercolor-and-ink comic interpreting Kagga 5 as an unsparing inquiry into God, ignorance, suffering, birth, and death. Do not resolve the questions with easy reassurance. Use the supplied Kagga 1 comic only as a character and art-direction reference. Do not reuse its golden-center composition.

Use one wide landscape page with exactly six equal pictures in a clean three-column by two-row grid, separated by narrow warm-paper gutters. Reading order is left to right across the top row, then the bottom row.

Mankuthimma is the same ordinary middle-aged Kannada householder and traveler: slightly untidy graying dark hair, modest moustache, weathered kindly face, attentive intelligent eyes, compact adult proportions, handwoven off-white cotton shirt, traditional panche/dhoti, faded earth-toned shoulder cloth, worn sandals, and practical cloth bag. He is humble, reflective, engaged in ordinary life, religion-agnostic, and spiritually curious without representing any faith—never a monk, guru, or renunciate. A tiny sparrow appears subtly in each panel.

1. Mankuthimma pauses on an ordinary journey at the mouth of a deep natural cave; its interior is dark and offers no visible figure or idol. Caption: “Is God a dark cave?”
2. Inside, indistinct natural shapes emerge from shadow—cloud, bone, root, star, wave—things the mind struggles to name, with no writing or symbols. Caption: “A name for what we cannot know?”
3. From the cave mouth Mankuthimma sees a high watchtower silhouette above a wide valley, suggesting a guardian, while ordinary life continues below. Caption: “If one guardian watches all...”
4. A storm damages one hut while another remains untouched; villagers help one another amid rain. Show hardship with restraint, no graphic injury. Caption: “Why does the world suffer so?”
5. In one continuous riverside scene, a mother cradles a newborn beneath a flowering tree while farther downstream mourners carry a covered bier toward a small funeral flame. Keep it dignified and non-graphic. Caption: “Why birth? Why death?”
6. At twilight Mankuthimma sits quietly at the cave threshold between the dark interior and the troubled, living valley after helping the villagers. The sparrow rests beside him. No revelation appears. This is the signature image. Caption: “The cave gave no easy answer.”

Use contemplative hand-painted watercolor with loose uneven ink outlines, transparent washes, visible paper texture, soft pigment blooms, and restrained emotion. Palette: off-white cotton, faded rust, muted indigo, cave charcoal, storm blue-gray, earth brown, muted green, small warm firelight, and violet twilight. Preserve warmth through human care, but do not make the suffering cute or solve it with supernatural light.

Critical constraint: exactly six pictures and exactly one English narration caption per picture. Render the six quoted captions verbatim, in order, in consistent warm-cream caption bands with large dark legible lettering. Preserve the three periods in panel 3 exactly. No Kannada, dialogue, speech bubbles, labels, title, panel numbers, logo, watermark, credits, signage, religious text, decorative script, pseudo-writing, or other text.
